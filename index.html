Welcome

New to AA?

Walking through the door for the first time takes courage. Here's what you can expect at Melrose — no surprises, no judgment, just people who understand.

You're Welcome Here

If you think you might have a problem with alcohol, you've come to the right place. The only requirement for AA membership is a desire to stop drinking — that's it. You don't need to sign up, pay anything, or make any commitments. Just show up.

The 511 Melrose building has been a home to AA meetings in Iowa City for decades. When you arrive, you'll find coffee, friendly faces, and people who have been exactly where you are. Many of us were nervous the first time too.

Our Monday 6 PM Beginner's Meeting is specifically designed for people who are new or returning to AA. It's a great place to start — but you're welcome at any meeting on the schedule.

What to Expect at Your First Meeting

Every meeting is a little different, but here's the general shape of things.

☗

Just Show Up

No registration, no appointment, no RSVP. Walk in, grab a cup of coffee, and find a seat. If you feel comfortable, let someone know it's your first time — they'll be glad to help you feel at home.

♪

Listening Is Enough

You will never be required to speak. Most meetings include a moment for newcomers to introduce themselves by first name only — but that's entirely optional. Many people attend their first several meetings quietly.

♥

What Happens in the Room

Meetings typically open with a reading, followed by sharing — one person at a time talking about their experience with alcoholism and recovery. Some meetings have a speaker, others are open discussion. All are confidential.

⚖

No Dues or Fees

AA is free. A basket is passed for voluntary contributions (our "7th Tradition"), but newcomers are asked not to contribute. We want you here regardless of your financial situation.

★

Everyone Is Welcome

AA welcomes people of all backgrounds — every age, race, gender, sexual orientation, religion (or none), and walk of life. The only thing we have in common is that we want to stop drinking.

↺

You Can Keep Coming Back

One meeting might change your life, or it might take a few to feel comfortable. That's normal. Try different meetings and times. Many people find that regular attendance is what makes the difference.

Meeting Types at Melrose

Different meetings serve different needs — here's a quick guide.

Open Meetings

Anyone is welcome — members, family, friends, or anyone curious about AA.

Closed Meetings

Reserved for people who have a desire to stop drinking. A safe space for deeper sharing.

Beginner's Meetings

Focused on Steps 1, 2, and 3. Questions encouraged. Experienced members help newcomers.

Big Book Study

Reading and discussing "Alcoholics Anonymous" chapter by chapter. Great for learning the program.

Discussion Meetings

A topic is introduced and members share their experience as it relates. The most common format.

Speaker Meetings

One or two members share their story — what it was like, what happened, and what it's like now.

Governance

Melrose Group Guidelines

How our group operates — chairperson expectations, Group Conscience structure, officer positions, and our safety commitment.

Each Alcoholics Anonymous Group ought to be a spiritual entity having but one primary purpose — to carry the message to the alcoholic who still suffers.

I. Meeting Chairpersons

  • All chairs should have three months of sobriety and have attended meetings for three months (excludes treatment meetings).
  • There is one numbered key for each meeting. When one person stops chairing and another starts, the key should transfer to the new person, and the Key Coordinator should be notified. Key numbers are on the whiteboard.
  • Every three months, there is an opportunity to rotate. The whiteboard will indicate who wants to take over for the next quarter. The spirit of rotation is encouraged.
  • It is suggested that the AA Melrose Chair Script be followed as written.
  • The chair should arrive thirty minutes before the start of the meeting to unlock the door, make coffee, and greet members as they arrive.
  • After each meeting, chairs are responsible for washing the dishes and coffee pots, turning off the coffee, making sure everyone is out of the building at the end of the meeting, and locking the door.
  • If there is a disruption during the meeting, you may ask the person to leave or get help from another alcoholic. It is not against AA traditions to call the police if safety is a concern.

II. Group Conscience

  • Group Conscience allows everyone to be an equal member of the group. We should not have designated or self-appointed "decision makers."
  • Any member that attends Group Conscience and considers Melrose Group their home group may vote.
  • Group Conscience meets once a month and may schedule additional meetings as needed. Members may attend in person or via Zoom (if available).
  • Members holding positions should have a report to share with the group.
  • Members who hold positions should arrive on time for each meeting. If unable to attend, please send a report to the Secretary at least 24 hours before the meeting.
  • If a member is absent more than 30% of meetings per year (without a report sent), Group Conscience may reconsider their membership and vote to replace that member.

III. Group Conscience Positions

For officer positions, we look for the same character qualities that mark a good trusted servant: patience, understanding, and a firm determination to place principles before personalities.

Group Conscience Chair — 2-year commitment · 2 years sobriety

  • Requires previous experience holding other position(s) on Group Conscience.
  • Creates an agenda for each meeting, facilitates the meetings, and coordinates activities with other group officers.
  • Communicates the news of Group Conscience meetings to the Melrose meetings.

Secretary — 2-year commitment · 2 years sobriety

  • Records the minutes during Group Conscience meetings, types up the minutes, and emails them to the board within a few days.
  • Prints minutes for the Melrose bulletin board.
  • Receives officer reports from those who cannot attend and reads them to the group.

Treasurer — 2-year commitment · 2 years sobriety

  • Creates a monthly financial report to print and distribute at Group Conscience.
  • After the group's basic needs are met (rent, literature, refreshments), participates in the financial support of the Fellowship by sending money to Intergroup, Area/District, and the General Service Board.
  • Documents all expenditures for literature, chips, coffee, and other group expenses.
  • Maintains prudent reserve.

Literature and Chips — 1-year commitment · 1 year sobriety

  • Tracks the need for books, pamphlets, and chips. Orders from AA.org when necessary and keeps invoices for reimbursement.

Intergroup Representative — 1-year commitment · 1 year sobriety

  • Attends all Intergroup meetings.
  • Represents Melrose Group, relaying current information between Melrose and Intergroup at each Group Conscience meeting.

Keys Coordinator — 2-year commitment · 1 year sobriety

  • Records key numbers for each meeting and ensures new chairs receive the key from previous chairs.
  • A record of key numbers and meeting chairs is written in the Key Binder. Each key costs the group $50, so lost or unaccounted-for keys are the responsibility of the chair who held it.

Webmaster — 2-year commitment · 1 year sobriety

  • Gathers needed passwords and logins for 511melrosegroup.org.
  • Maintains website, adding current events, Melrose news, and current meeting minutes.
  • Makes meeting adjustments on the website when meetings are added, removed, or times change.

GSR (General Service Representative) — 2-year commitment · 2 years sobriety

  • Active participation in Melrose Group is required, with a good understanding of the 12 Traditions.
  • The GSR is Melrose Group's link to AA as a whole. Attends district meetings and area assemblies.
  • Sees to it that Melrose Group's conscience on matters of importance becomes a part of the consensus at the annual Conference meeting.

Alternate GSR

  • Attends district meetings when the GSR is unavailable.
  • Works closely with the GSR to be a knowledgeable replacement.

IV. Safety Card (from GSO)

A.A. membership does not include having to tolerate sexual harassment, threats of violence, financial coercion, or bullying. Nor is pressuring A.A. members into a particular point of view or belief relating to medication, religion, and other outside issues acceptable.

No one should have to tolerate racial, sexual orientation, gender, age, or other discrimination when they seek help from AA.

It is not against A.A. tradition to call the police or discuss an incident with a sponsor, Group Conscience member, or trusted friend for guidance.

Anonymity is not a cloak protecting criminal behavior. The same laws that apply to the public also apply to AA meetings.

Please tell the meeting chairperson or another trusted member in the room if you need support.
